## Answer (mock built; waiting for the instructor's review)

The mock is `.scratch/milestone-5-landscape/mock/index.html` (open it in a browser; `build.py`, `landscape.js` and `shots.mjs` next to it make it and its screenshots, all thrown away afterwards). The buttons along the top switch screen (landscape view, crossover, mutation, binary-coded mutation, menu, drawer), function, representation (real, plain binary, Gray), crossover operator, trails (off, 3, 5 generations), stage marks (parents, children, mutated child), 12 or 60 individuals, the surface rotation, the restart notice, light/dark, normal/projector. Screenshots of 19 states at 1920×1080 are in `mock/screenshots/`. The data are hand-made; the heatmap, isolines, surface (a real perspective mesh with shading and hidden-marker detection) and the numbers in the operator cards are computed.

**What the mock proposes, and what to decide**

1. **Where it lives** (`01`): the stage panel's second view "Stage view | Landscape" with the narration bar still visible; a control row (trails off / 1 / 3 / 5 generations, rotate buttons ◀ ▶ ▲ ▼ reset), the heatmap and the surface side by side (520 × 296 px each), a key line, and a sentence for screen readers. *Decision: keep?*
2. **Heatmap**: the perceptual ramp **viridis** (dark purple = worst, yellow = best, colour-blind safe) with a labelled scale, **isolines at 20, 40, 60 and 80 % of the fitness range with their values** as the non-colour carrier, axes with ticks, optimum marks ✕ with a label ("optimum 1 … 4" for Himmelblau). For the minimised functions the caption reads "fitness = −f(x, y), higher is better" and the scale runs from −890 to 0. *Decision: ramp and isoline levels OK?*
3. **Surface**: a shaded 44 × 44 mesh in perspective, fixed light, floor frame with x and y labels, optimum marks on the surface; markers stand on it and one hidden behind a ridge becomes a **dashed ring without its id**; rotate by dragging, by the arrow keys on the focused view, or by the buttons. *Decision: mesh, shading and the dashed ring for hidden markers OK?*
4. **Markers** (same on both views): ● individual with its id, ◆ elite, ★ best of the generation, ▢ parent (with the letter A or B), ▲ child (O1, O2), ✕ optimum, a dashed ring for hidden, an **orange ring with a crosshair for the selected individual** (also highlighted in the table: ▸ and a tinted row), small faded dots for trails; stage marks as in `05` (parents), `06` (children with their id) and `07` (a mutated child with an orange arrow from a dashed ring where it was). *Decision: shapes and the stage marks OK?*
5. **The population is a small cluster on a big map**: with 12 individuals and the default spread the markers overlap in the corner of the heatmap (see `01`, `05`, `07`), which makes ids hard to read. I suggest a **"Zoom to population" toggle** on the heatmap (rescale to the bounding box of the population plus a margin, axes relabelled, off by default). It adds a control and some scope. *Decision: add it, or leave it to the selection and the table?* My recommendation: add it.
6. **Population table**: for the real-valued representation the pair "(−2.505, −1.755)" (a column "Chromosome (x, y)") and Fitness, plus f(x, y) with the note "= −f" for the minimised functions (`04`); for binary-coded the bits grouped by variable under a variable row "x · 10 bits | y · 10 bits" (like the trap's block row) with decoded x, y columns and Fitness (`08`, `09`). Numbers are right-aligned with three decimals and a real minus sign. *Decision: OK?*
7. **Crossover cards** (`11`, `12`, `13`): **BLX-α** shows, per gene, the parents ●, the parents' interval (purple), the widened interval (dashed orange), the draw and the child ▲ on a number line, with the numbers printed, and "▮ clamped to the bound" where a draw was clamped; **arithmetic** shows λ, a table of A, B, O1 = λ·A + (1 − λ)·B and O2, and a number line; **discrete** a table with the swapped gene highlighted. *Decision: OK?*
8. **Mutation cards**: Gaussian (`14`): per gene the draw u against pm, for a mutating gene the two uniform draws, z, the step σ·range·z, the value after and "▮ clamped (was …, upper bound …)". Binary-coded (`15`): the same value shown in plain binary and in Gray, and for each bit what flipping it does to x ("moves by"): plain binary needs 10 flips to step from the value just below the middle to the next one, Gray code one, and the table shows the sizes (+5.005 against +0.010 for the move across the middle). *Decision: OK? (This is the "cliff" demonstration.)*
9. **Bottom strip**: the fitness plot with the optimum line (for minimised functions "optimum 0 (f = 0)" and "fitness = −f"); the diversity plot (mean distance in decoded space as a share of the diagonal, and for binary-coded a dotted ◇ Hamming line, the legend wraps in the mock and will be shortened); a **Spread** panel with the standard deviations of x and y as bars (share of each range) and a history line. *Decision: OK?*
10. **Drawer** (`16`, `17`): landscape, representation, bits per variable for the binary-coded ones, the crossover types of the chosen representation (BLX-α with its α field only for real-valued), mutation σ for real-valued or the bit-flip mode, and "Stop within ε"; the restart notice for a representation switch lists exactly what resets ("crossover type BLX-α → One-point; mutation Gaussian σ = 0.05 → bit flip, per gene, pm 0.05"). **Menu** (`18`): Landscape is the fifth entry, logo `Ls`. *Decision: OK?*
