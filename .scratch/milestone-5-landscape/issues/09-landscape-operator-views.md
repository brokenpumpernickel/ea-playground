# 09 Operator views: real genes, and decoded values for binary-coded ones

Status: resolved
Type: task (test-first)
Blocked by: 08
Spec: ../spec.md

## What to build

As decided in the mock: the crossover card for **BLX-α** (per gene the interval, the widened interval and the draw), **arithmetic** (λ, both results with the weights) and **discrete** operators on real pairs, with children as pairs and clamped genes marked; the mutation card for **Gaussian** (per gene u, and for a mutating gene the two uniforms, z, the step σ × range × z, before and after, clamping marked), per gene and per individual; and for binary-coded chromosomes the existing crossover and mutation views with each chromosome followed by its decoded (x, y) and, for a child or a mutated gene, how far the point moved in decoded space, in plain binary and in Gray. Pure models first (read from the recorded events only), then the views; bypasses shown as bypasses; the narration sentences.

## Done when

- Models equal a direct recomputation from the events (every number shown is an event's number or a function of them); property tests over generated runs for all operators and representations; rendered checks and geometric tests at both sizes at every micro-step of two generations (no overlap, no clipping, widths stable), with the narration inside its box.
- Other problems' views are unchanged.

## Answer
The crossover and mutation views of the landscape, in all three representations, and the sentences that narrate them.

- **Models** (`variation-models.ts`, read from the recorded events only; `Display` now carries the landscape): a pair gets `blend` (per gene: the parents, d, the widened interval, both draws, raw and clamped values, the children's genes) for BLX-α and `lambda` for arithmetic crossover, the exact numbers of parents and children (the text rounds them), the genes each child had clamped, and for a binary-coded landscape the point every parent and child decodes to and each child's distance from each parent; a mutation row gets, for a real landscape, every gene's draw, whether it mutated and for a mutating gene u₁, u₂, z, σ × range × z, raw, after and clamping, and for a binary-coded one the point before and after and how far it moved (x, y and in all). The explanations name α, λ, σ, the range, z and the clamping.
- **Cards**: BLX-α per gene on a **number line** (`NumberLine.svelte`: dashed band for the widened interval, solid band between the parents, circles for A and B, triangles for O1 and O2, a bar where a child was cut to a bound; the numbers are in the text beside it, so the drawing is hidden from screen readers) with "draws u = 0.469 → O1 1.778 · u = 0.946 → O2 5.000 ▮ clamped to 5.000 (the draw gave 12.654)"; arithmetic with λ, a table of A, B, O1 = λ·A + (1 − λ)·B and O2 and a line per gene; the discrete operators on real pairs keep the two-column parents-and-children layout with the pairs (a dashed cut after x, hatching for genes from B); children as pairs with a clamped gene marked ▮. Gaussian mutation: per offspring a table of gene, before, draw (u against pm, or "chosen" with the decision draw above it for per-individual), the two uniforms and z, the step σ·range·z with its numbers, and after with clamping, then the result pair; bypasses are bypasses. For the binary-coded ones the existing views gain "→ (x, y)" after each chromosome, "d from A · d from B" after each child, "moved d (x ±…, y ±…)" after a mutated offspring, the per-individual mutation of long strings uses the card layout, and a collapsible **flip table** shows, for the same x in plain binary and in Gray code, what flipping each bit does (x becomes, moves by) and in how many bits neighbouring values differ.
- **Narration** (`describe.ts`): "Pair 2: u = 0.427 < pc 1 → recombined (BLX-α, α = 2): children (1.778, 3.506) and (5.000, 3.286) · clamped to a bound: O4 x", "… (arithmetic, λ = 0.25) …", "Offspring 2: gene x drew u = 0.031 < pm 0.15 → z = 0.435, step +0.218 (4.620 → 4.838): (4.620, −1.384) → (4.838, −1.384)", the per-individual, two-gene, unchanged and bypass forms, and for the binary-coded ones "…; the point moves from (…) to (…) by d"; the evaluation and setup sentences for a landscape no longer say the point twice and round fitness to three decimals (they showed 17 digits).
