# 12 The population on the views: markers, selection, stage marks, trails

Status: resolved
Type: task (test-first)
Blocked by: 09, 11
Spec: ../spec.md

## What to build

The population on both the heatmap and the surface, as decided in the mock: a marker per individual at its decoded point (shape and outline, not colour alone) with its id; elites, the best individual of the generation, a selected individual (a click on a marker, a click on a table row or the keyboard equivalent highlights it on both views and in the table); the stage marks while stepping (the parents of the current pair, the children made so far, a mutated child with an arrow from before to after, clamped genes noted); **trails** of the last 1, 3 or 5 generations as fading marks (off by default); a text alternative (a sentence of where the population is and where the best is, and a hidden table of positions and fitness); markers hidden when behind a ridge on the surface. Follows the history cursor.

## Done when

- The marker positions equal the recorded populations (data attributes) for every generation shown, in both representations; the highlighted individual is the same in the table and on both views; stage marks equal the events at the cursor; trails equal earlier populations; no marker is drawn outside the plot.
- Keyboard operation of the selection; no control without a name; reduced motion; geometric tests at both sizes at every micro-step of two generations with 12 and 60 individuals.

## Answer
The population on both landscape views, with selection, stage marks and trails.

- **Marks model** (`landscape-marks.ts`, pure, read from the run at the cursor): one mark per individual of the population the table shows (an elite carried over is a diamond, the best of the generation a star, the rest circles), the parents of the stage under way (selection: those picked so far; pairing: the pairs made so far; crossover: the current pair as squares marked A and B, "AB" when one individual was picked twice), the offspring made so far (crossover: as pairs are made, with the genes cut to a bound; mutation: where mutation left them, the current mutated one with the point it came from for the arrow, and a clamped gene noted; evaluation: those scored), trails (the positions of the last 1, 3 or 5 generations before the one shown, oldest first, none at the start), the selected individual, a sentence ("Generation 2: 10 individuals; the best is individual 8 at (…) with fitness …; the population spans x from … to … and y from … to …. Individual 5 is selected: …") and a table of positions with each individual's role. `layoutMarks` turns that into shapes at pixels for a picture: a diamond, star, square (with its letter) or circle, triangles for offspring, labels for every individual up to 16 and beyond that only the ones that matter, trails fading with age, nothing outside the plot, and a flag for a mark hidden behind a ridge (dashed ring, no label).
- **Layer and views** (`MarkerLayer.svelte`, `Heatmap.svelte`, `Surface.svelte`): an SVG over each canvas, so every mark is an element with `data-id`, `data-shape`, the decoded `data-x`/`data-y` and its pixel; shapes and labels carry the meaning, not colour; the selected one has a ring and a cross; the layer is hidden from screen readers (the sentence and a table of positions speak for it) except that the heatmap's layer takes the keyboard. The surface places a marker at its fitness a hair above the surface and asks the item buffer whether a ridge covers it. Dragging the surface now captures the pointer only after a few pixels of movement, so a click on a marker is not swallowed.
- **Selection** (`session.selectIndividual`, `selectedId`): a click on a marker on either view, a click on a table row's id button ("Select individual 24", `aria-pressed`), or the heatmap's focused layer (left and right arrows through the individuals, wrapping; Home and End; Escape clears) highlights that individual on both views, in the table (row highlighted) and in the sentence; selecting the same one again clears it; it starts over with the run and survives stepping. The table's id cell for a landscape holds that button; the column is 112 px so the parent marks do not move it.
- **Trails**: a toolbar group "Trails: off, 1 gen, 3 gens, 5 gens" (`aria-pressed`, off by default, a view setting that starts over with the run), the same dots on both views.
- **Layout**: the toolbar fits on one row (Zoom, Trails, Rotate), the symbol key is one line, and the picture sentence and the population sentence share one small paragraph; the two canvases are 520 × 270 (`landscape-canvas.ts`, one constant for the heatmap, the surface and their tests; the surface projection still shows the whole floor).
- **Tests**: `landscape-marks.test.ts` (23: marks equal the recorded populations in every generation for the real pair and both codings; elites are the replacement's; the best; the parents and letters per micro-step against the pairing and crossover events including "AB"; offspring and their order against the events; clamped genes; mutation positions, the arrow's `from` and clamped steps; evaluation; no children outside the offspring stages; trails of every length, oldest first, following the cursor; the sentence and the table; the label rule; layout shapes, labels, arrows, hiding, trail fading and nothing outside the plot), `session-landscape.test.ts` (5) and `e2e/landscape-marks.spec.ts` (16): markers equal the engine's populations on both views for generations 0 to 3 in all three representations (elites diamonds, the best a star), heatmap positions equal the pixels the heatmap gives, selection by click on either view, by table button and by keyboard (and the same individual on both views, in the table and in the sentence), the stage marks at every micro-step of a generation's crossover and mutation against the events (squares A and B, triangles as made, the arrow, clamped genes), trails for 1, 3 and 5 generations with fading and the cursor, the sentence and table following the history cursor, hidden markers on the surface equal to the projection's verdict, every control named, reduced motion with no running animation, and at 1920×1080 and 1366×768 in projector mode with 12 and 60 individuals at every micro-step of two generations: the view fits its panel, the toolbar stays on one row, every mark is inside its picture, the narration is clear.
