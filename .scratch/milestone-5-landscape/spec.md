# Milestone 5: the two-dimensional landscape

Status: broken into tickets (17), see `issues/`

Vocabulary follows `CONTEXT.md` (including the new **Real-valued chromosome**, **Bounds**, **Blend crossover (BLX-α)**, **Arithmetic crossover**, **Gaussian mutation**, **Landscape**, **Heatmap**, **Surface** and **Trail**). Architecture follows ADRs 0001–0005 and the new **ADR 0006** (real-valued chromosomes). Where this spec and the ADRs or glossary disagree, they win and this spec is wrong. Milestones 1–4 are complete and published; this milestone builds on the problem registry, the problem-declared columns, the contexts that give views problem-specific information, the schema tracker's pattern of a second view in the stage panel, and the share-link configuration.

## Problem

The four problems so far have discrete genes. Students also need to see an algorithm move through a *continuous* search space: how a population spreads over a landscape, climbs, clusters on several hills, loses diversity and converges, sometimes on the wrong hill, and what the real-valued operators they will implement next (BLX-α, arithmetic crossover, Gaussian mutation) actually do to a population.

## Goal

A fifth problem in which students evolve points (x, y) on a two-dimensional fitness landscape, watch the population on a **heatmap** and on a rotatable **surface** of the same landscape, step through every operator with its arithmetic visible, and see movement, clustering, diversity and premature convergence, including runs that end on the wrong hill. Higher fitness is always better, in height, colour, table and plot, whatever the underlying function does. The same landscape can be searched with either of two representations, chosen in the setup: a **real-valued chromosome** (two numbers, with BLX-α, arithmetic crossover and Gaussian mutation) or a **binary-coded chromosome** (each variable written in bits, in plain binary or Gray code, with the bit operators of the earlier problems), so that students can compare how the representation changes what the operators do to the same points.

## Scope

### In

**Representation and operators (ADR 0006)**
- **Representation** is a setup option (so changing it is a reset): **Real-valued**, **Binary-coded (plain binary)** or **Binary-coded (Gray code)**. The same function, bounds, selection, elitism, stop criteria and views serve all three.
- **Real-valued**: two real genes (x, y) with the function's bounds. Random initialization is uniform inside the bounds. Hand-edited populations are typed as pairs of numbers.
  - **Crossover**: one-point, two-point and uniform (discrete recombination: each child gene is a copy of a parent's gene; with two genes one-point swaps y and two-point is not available), **BLX-α** (per gene: a uniform draw from [min − α·d, max + α·d], d = |a − b|; α is a parameter, default 0.5) and **whole arithmetic crossover** (children λ·A + (1 − λ)·B and (1 − λ)·A + λ·B, one λ per pair, drawn uniformly from [0, 1]).
  - **Mutation**: **Gaussian**, per gene (each gene mutates with probability pm) or per individual (with probability pm one gene, chosen at random, mutates), the step being a normal draw times σ × the gene's range (σ is a parameter, default 0.05).
  - **Boundary handling**: values that leave the bounds are clamped, and the events and views say which genes were clamped.
- **Binary-coded**: the chromosome is 2·n bits (the x bits, then the y bits; n = **bits per variable**, 4 to 12, default 10), alphabet `0, 1`, so every operator, view and rule of the binary problems applies unchanged: one-point, two-point and uniform crossover, and bit-flip mutation per gene or per individual. A variable's value is decoded as lo + (hi − lo) · v / (2ⁿ − 1), where v is the integer the n bits spell in plain binary, or the integer the Gray code decodes to (standard reflected Gray code) when Gray is chosen. Hand-edited populations are typed as bit strings of 2·n bits. The real-only operators are not offered.
- **What applies when**: the drawer offers only the operators that exist for the chosen representation (crossover types, and for mutation Gaussian σ or bit-flip), and the parameters of the other representation are hidden. Switching representation restarts the run and resets the crossover type and the mutation settings to the new representation's defaults; the restart notice says exactly what is reset. A link or preset with an operator that does not exist for its representation is refused with a readable message.
- Selection (roulette with the weight chain, tournament), elitism, truncation, replacement, stop criteria and history work unchanged on the fitness numbers in all three representations.

**Landscapes** (chosen in the drawer's run setup; part of the problem's options, so reset-needed and carried by links and presets; the bounds belong to the function)
- **Single peak** (maximised): f = exp(−((x − 2)² + (y + 1)²) / 8) on [−5, 5]²; optimum 1 at (2, −1). Smooth, for seeing convergence.
- **Two peaks** (maximised): a broad lower hill and a narrow tall one, on [−5, 5]²: f = 0.6·exp(−((x + 2.5)² + (y + 2.5)²) / 8) + exp(−((x − 3)² + (y − 3)²) / 0.5). The global optimum is the narrow hill (value within 1e-3 of 1, found numerically and asserted by a test), the broad hill is the trap for premature convergence.
- **Rastrigin** (minimised): f = 20 + Σ (x² − 10·cos(2πx)) over x, y on [−5.12, 5.12]²; minimum 0 at (0, 0), many local minima. Fitness = −f.
- **Himmelblau** (minimised): f = (x² + y − 11)² + (x + y² − 7)² on [−5, 5]²; four equal minima of 0 at (3, 2), (−2.805118, 3.131312), (−3.779310, −3.283186) and (3.584428, −1.848126). Fitness = −f.
- For the minimised ones the screen shows the objective f(x, y), the rule "fitness = −f" in the explanation and the column header, and the surface, heatmap, colours and plots are always of **fitness** (higher is better, with the colour scale labelled). Roulette selection uses the weight chain's existing Shift for negative fitness and says so, as it does elsewhere.
- Each function has a known optimum fitness (and for Himmelblau four optimum points), asserted by tests (analytic where possible, a fine grid search and local refinement otherwise).

**UI**
- The problem menu gains **Landscape** (logo `Ls`) with its own defaults, seed and presets, through the existing restart notice.
- **Population table columns:** x, y (three decimals; full precision in the data), the objective f(x, y) (only for minimised functions, where it differs from fitness), Fitness. For a real-valued chromosome the cell shows the pair "(2.134, −0.871)" with its accessible name, not bits. For a binary-coded one the cell shows the bits grouped by variable (the x bits, a gap, the y bits, with a variable row above like the trap's block row) and the columns x and y show the decoded values, so students read bits and meaning side by side.
- **Landscape view**, a second view of the stage panel ("Stage view | Landscape", as the schema tracker is for the trap), with the narration bar still visible so stepping makes sense:
  - **Heatmap** of fitness over the bounds with a labelled colour scale (a perceptual ramp with a non-colour carrier: isolines at fixed fitness levels with their values), axes with ticks, and the optimum marked (×, with its label; four marks for Himmelblau).
  - **Surface** of the same landscape as a shaded height field in perspective, drawn on a canvas by this app (no dependency; the single-file `file://` build is unchanged), rotated by dragging or by the arrow keys on the focused view (with buttons for left, right, up, down and reset), the population standing on it as markers that are hidden when behind a ridge, the optimum marked.
  - **The population on both**: each individual a marker with its id (shape and outline, not only colour); elites ◆ marked, the best individual of the generation marked, a selected individual highlighted on both views and in the table (click a marker or a table row, or the keyboard equivalent), and while stepping, the individuals the current stage deals with are marked: parents of the current pair, the children produced so far, mutated children with an arrow from before to after, clamped genes noted.
  - **Zoom to population**: a toggle on the heatmap (off by default) that rescales it to the bounding box of the population plus a margin, with the axes relabelled, so a tight cluster can be read (decided in the mock).
  - **Trails**: an option to show the last 1, 3 or 5 generations of the population as fading marks (the individuals' positions only, not lineage), off by default.
  - A text and table alternative for both views (the hidden table of positions and fitness; a sentence saying where the population is and where the best is).
- **Operator views** for binary-coded chromosomes are the existing crossover and mutation views, each chromosome followed by its decoded (x, y) and, for a child or a mutated gene, how far the point moved in decoded space (the plain-binary "cliff": one flipped high bit moves a point half the range, the same flip in Gray code a small step, is shown by the numbers, not asserted by the text). Operator views for real genes: the crossover card shows parents A and B as pairs, the operator and its draws (BLX-α: for each gene the interval and the draw; arithmetic: λ and both results; discrete: the cut or mask as for symbols) and the children as pairs with clamped genes marked; the mutation card shows per gene the draw u, and for a mutating gene the normal draw, the step σ × range × z and the before/after, with clamping marked; bypasses are shown as bypasses.
- **Metrics (both representations, so they compare):** best, average and worst fitness as before; **diversity** is the mean pairwise distance in decoded space normalised by the diagonal of the bounds (0 to 1), the diversity plot's label says so; the strip beside it shows the population's **spread** (standard deviation of x and of y as a share of each range) in place of the allele panel; "distinct" counts distinct points. For a binary-coded run the Hamming diversity of the earlier problems is also available as a second line of the diversity plot, labelled.
- **Drawer setup**: function, representation (and bits per variable for the binary-coded ones), and for the run parameters the crossover operator (with α for BLX-α) and the mutation settings (σ and the mode for real-valued, the bit-flip mode and probability for binary-coded) of the chosen representation, the usual probabilities, elitism, selection, stop criteria; stop criterion "target fitness reached" is **within ε of the optimum fitness** (ε a parameter, default 1e-3 of the fitness range of the function, shown), since an exact optimum is never reached by real numbers.
- Everything from milestones 1–4's accessibility pass applies: real tables, a name and a sentence for each individual, a non-colour carrier for every colour-coded meaning (the audit list extended), keyboard operation (including the surface and the marker selection), reduced motion (no rotation animation, no trail fading), projector mode, no overflow at 1920×1080 and 1366×768 for every stage, the landscape view, the drawer and the menu.

**Share links and presets**
- Options: the function, the representation and, for the binary-coded ones, the bits per variable. Parameters: the operator, α, σ and ε as for any parameter. The link carries a hand-edited population (pairs of numbers) exactly. Every bad-option case gets a readable message.
- **Presets** (data files, each seed verified by a test that asserts what its text says), candidates to be settled by measurement: **Single peak** (converges quickly), **Two peaks, premature convergence** (a seed that settles on the broad hill, and the statistics over seeds), **Rastrigin** (many local minima: the population splits across them), **Gray against plain binary** (the same seed and settings on the same landscape: where plain binary stalls at a Hamming cliff and Gray code climbs, only if a real seed shows it), **Real against binary-coded** (the same landscape and population size: what each representation does with a ridge, only if measured), **Himmelblau** (four optima: clustering), **Mutation too small** (σ so small the population stalls), **Arithmetic crossover** (the population contracts toward its centre; diversity falls fast), **BLX-α** (α = 0.5 against α = 0: with α = 0 the children stay inside the parents' box, so the search cannot expand). A preset expected to fail says so.

**Defaults** (measured over at least 20 seeds before the instructor approves, as before): population size, selection, crossover operator, α, σ, pm and pc, elites, limits and a stagnation window measured so it does not pause a run that is still moving, so that the default function finds its optimum in most but not all runs and the harder functions show the deceptive behaviour.

### Out

- User-defined functions or bounds; a mixed chromosome of both representations; other codings (Gray is the only alternative to plain binary); more than two variables; constraints; multi-objective views.
- Evolution strategies (self-adaptive σ), differential evolution, CMA: later algorithms. Other real-valued operators (simulated binary crossover, polynomial mutation).
- Lineage lines across generations (only trails of positions); animated interpolation between generations (markers jump, with trails showing where they came from).
- Any change to the run layer, history, playback or the share-link format beyond new options and parameters.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **Existing problems untouched.** All milestone 1–4 tests pass, the golden runs reproduce exactly (populations, events, original metrics), and the other four problems' screens, plots and links are unchanged.
2. **Landscape functions and decoding.** Binary-coded decoding is checked on hand-worked bit strings in plain binary and Gray (the end points map to the bounds exactly; consecutive integers map to evenly spaced values; adjacent Gray words differ in one bit and decode to adjacent values; plain binary 0111…1 and 1000…0 are adjacent values with the largest Hamming distance), and the real and binary-coded problems give the same fitness at the same decoded point. Values checked on hand-worked points for each function; each known optimum (and Himmelblau's four) verified numerically; bounds as stated; fitness = f or −f as stated; every individual of every run is inside the bounds.
3. **Operators.** For binary-coded chromosomes the operators are the existing ones and their tests apply; the representation's operator rules are enforced (a real-only operator for a binary-coded problem, or the reverse, is refused with a readable message in the drawer and in links). For real-valued: hand-worked cases and property tests: BLX-α children lie in the widened interval and inside the bounds after clamping; α = 0 gives children inside the parents' box; arithmetic crossover gives λ·A + (1 − λ)·B and its mirror, with λ recorded; Gaussian mutation step equals σ × range × z with z from the two recorded uniform draws (Box–Muller, documented order); clamping is recorded and exact; per-gene and per-individual probabilities and bypasses behave as for the symbol operators; discrete operators work on real vectors; no two individuals share a chromosome array.
4. **Reproducibility and agreement.** A seeded landscape run is identical by micro-step, stage, generation, run-until and every playback tier; replaying a generation's events reproduces the population; every event's numbers agree with the displayed operator views.
5. **Views agree with the engine.** The marker positions on the heatmap and surface equal the recorded populations (data attributes), the optimum marks equal the known optima, the highlighted individual is the same on the table and both views, stage marks equal the events at the cursor, trails equal earlier populations; the surface's projection is unit-tested (a point projects where the maths says; a hidden point is hidden).
6. **Links and presets.** Every preset of all five problems and generated landscape configurations (functions, operators with α and σ, hand-edited populations) round-trip through links with run equality; every bad-option case gives a readable message and applies nothing; each preset's text is verified by a test on its seed.
7. **Accessibility.** The audit table extended and checked, keyboard-only operation of the surface and marker selection, no unnamed controls, reduced motion, projector mode, no overflow or overlap at both sizes including the landscape view.
8. **Hosting and review.** The published site opens a landscape link as the same run; in a real browser at 1920×1080 in projector mode a full generation can be stepped through with the landscape view open, every label is legible, no colour-coded meaning relies on colour alone; the layout is also checked at 1366×768; the instructor signs off.

## Verification approach

- Golden runs stay the safety net and are run before and after the engine change.
- Vitest, test-first through public interfaces: the functions and optima, the operators and their events, the real-valued initialization, formatting and parsing, metrics and stops, the codec, the presets, the projection and hidden-point logic, the view models.
- Property tests over generated runs: everything inside the bounds, replay equals live, the operator identities above.
- Playwright for scripted scenarios, with geometry tests at both sizes and at every micro-step of several generations (the lessons of milestones 3 and 4: overflow of rows inside cards, widths that do not move, narration inside its box, run with the extreme data), a rendered check of the surface by reading pixels at the marker positions where meaningful, and screenshots reviewed by a person.
- Measurement scripts (not kept) for the defaults, α, σ and the preset seeds, with the numbers recorded in the tickets.
