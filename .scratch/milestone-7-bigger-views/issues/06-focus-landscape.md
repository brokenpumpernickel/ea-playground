# 06 The landscape in the focus view and its zoom

Status: resolved
Type: task (test-first)
Blocked by: 03, 04 (resolved)
Spec: ../spec.md

## What to build

The heatmap and the surface side by side, each about twice as large, with the same overlays (toolbar, figures card with the function, small fitness graph). Wheel zoom and drag on the heatmap (composing with Zoom to population; Reset view returns to the whole domain), wheel zoom on the surface (the camera comes closer), Reset view for both, the same keys.

## Done when

- The marks, selection and trails equal the engine's in the focus view; wheel, drag, keys and the buttons on both figures; zoom to population still works and composes; the overlays equal the metrics and stay inside the window at both sizes and in projector mode; Run changes the picture at every generation; keyboard only.

## Answer

**Layout** (`LandscapeView.svelte`): in the focus view the heatmap and the surface stand side by side, each canvas 725 × 410 design px instead of 520 × 270 (2.1 times the area), at the top of the body; the toolbar, the figures card and the fitness graph lie over the corners exactly as on the route map, and none of them touches a figure (a test compares the boxes at every micro-step of two generations). Under the figures, in the free space beside the graph, the two sentences (the landscape's, with its function, optima and region, and the population's) are written out. The **figures card** has the two statistics lines of the landscape (generation, best now, best so far, average, worst, evaluations: the same `statsLines` the ordinary panel prints), the function's name with its goal (minimised, so fitness = −f) and the key of the marks. The **graph** is the same component as on the map (the optimum line, no nearest-neighbour line). The toolbar adds **+**, **−** and **Reset view** (for both figures) and the **Graph** toggle to the existing buttons; Back to the panels is at its left.

**Zoom of the heatmap** (`heat-zoom.ts`, pure and unit-tested; the arithmetic of the map's zoom is reused): the view is a zoom and a place over a plot of 1000 × 1000 units, so it means the same part of the picture at any size (and in and out of the focus view); `subRegion` turns it into the part of the plane to draw on top of the region the buttons chose, so **the wheel and Zoom to population compose** (the wheel zooms inside the population's box, or inside the whole landscape); the wheel zooms about the pointer, a drag moves the picture (with the 4 px threshold the surface uses, so a click on a mark still selects), a double-click returns to the region the buttons chose, and from the keyboard on the marks `+` `−` `0` zoom and reset and `Shift`+arrows move the picture (the plain arrows still select individuals). The axes are relabelled and the marks follow the region as for Zoom to population (a mark outside the region is not drawn). The wheel and the keys also work in the ordinary view; the buttons only in the focus view, where the toolbar has the room.

**Zoom of the surface** (`surface-projection.ts`): the view gained an optional `zoom` (1× to 4×) that brings the camera closer: the wheel zooms, `+` `−` `0` on the focused surface zoom and reset, drag and the arrow keys still turn it and keep the zoom, the reset button and **Reset view** return to the first camera (az, el and zoom). **Reset view** also switches Zoom to population off and returns the heatmap to the whole domain, so it returns everything to the whole landscape.

**Tests:** unit (`heat-zoom.test.ts` 7, `surface-zoom.test.ts` 4): the part shown at 1×, 2× and 40×, the point under the pointer kept for the whole domain and for the population's region, the axes the right way up, a drag never leaving the region, the pointer held at the plot's edge; the camera zoom limited, kept by rotating and dragging, reset, and every point exactly twice as far from the middle at 2×. End-to-end (`e2e/focus-landscape.spec.ts`, 15): the figures about twice the area, in the body, apart from the three overlays and from each other, and back to their first size; the card equals the engine's figures at generations 0 to 4 and the graph equals the metrics (marker values, name equal to the hidden full plot's); the marks are the engine's on both figures in every generation (every individual at its decoded point, the surface's as well) and a click on the heatmap selects on the surface; wheel about the pointer (the point of the plane under it stays), a drag, the wheel out to the whole domain, the double-click; zoom to population and the wheel compose and Reset view returns everything; the surface wheel (1× to 4×), drag and arrows still turning, `+` `−` `0`; the heatmap's keys; every toolbar control; the zoom kept through steps, a restart and in and out of the focus view (the same region); **during Run the table, the graph and the marks agree on the generation in every drawn frame, every generation from 1 to 8 is drawn and at least six distinct sets of marks are seen**; geometry at both sizes in projector mode and not at every fourth micro-step of two generations and zoomed; keyboard-only reach of the controls, every one named, Esc leaves. Mutations (no surface wheel, no heatmap keys, a flipped axis) fail tests. 2,264 unit tests and `npm run check` pass; 960 end-to-end tests pass (43 skipped without their switches).

### Revision after the instructor's look at the first version

The first layout left a lot of the area empty (small figures, the sentence under them, the graph and the card in blank corners). Changed on the instructor's request: the long sentence under the figures ("Single peak: fitness …") is no longer on the screen (it stays for the screen reader); **both figures fill the height of the body** (892 × 620 design px each, about four times the ordinary area) and half its width each; the **fitness graph is a closable overlay on the heatmap** (inside its plot, bottom left) and the **information box a closable overlay on the surface** (top right), each with its own "× hide" and a toggle in the toolbar (**Graph**, **Info**, both with ✓ and `aria-pressed`), kept as left through steps and leaving; the toolbar stays above the figures. The geometry tests now say: the figures fill the body, the toolbar is apart from them, the graph lies inside the heatmap's box and the card inside the surface's, at both sizes in projector mode, every function and both representations; a test closes and reopens each box. 2,267 unit tests and the end-to-end suite pass.

### Second revision: movable overlays

On the instructor's request the fitness graph and the information box can be moved. A small **⠿ handle** in the header of each (`MoveHandle.svelte`, geometry in `overlay-drag.ts`, unit-tested) moves its overlay with the pointer or, from the keyboard, with the arrow keys (20 px, Shift 100 px) while the handle has the focus; Home or a double-click on it puts the overlay back; its name says so. The overlay is held inside the figure it lies on (the graph inside the heatmap, the card inside the surface) and, on the route map, below the toolbar; if it grows while it lies at an edge (a stage's longer text) it is pulled back in. Positions are view settings: kept through steps, restarts, hiding and showing, and leaving and entering the focus view, and started over by another problem. Done for the route map's graph and card as well, for consistency. `e2e/focus-move.spec.ts` (9 tests): drag to where it is put, cannot leave its figure in any direction, the other overlay unmoved, kept through the events above, Home and double-click, the keyboard (arrows do not step the run), the picture under it not moved or selected, the card growing at the bottom of the map, every handle named, reduced motion.
